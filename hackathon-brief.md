# Track 3: Ground Control

## Crew & Safety Operations

**Three hundred volunteers, a hot December weekend, and incidents that
don't wait.**

Fieldday's club nights run on about ten casual staff and a security
contractor. Rosters live in a group chat; incidents come in by radio and
get written up the next morning. Riverside needs 300 volunteers across
three days, with the right skills in the right places, and a safety team
that can see what's happening across a whole riverfront site.

Your job is to build something that keeps every shift covered and helps
Mo respond well when things change. **Every decision about people's
safety stays with a person.**

## 🚦 Where to Start

To get going, picture a real moment rather than a roster. For example:

> **2pm Saturday, 38°C.** Two first-aid volunteers haven't shown up, and
> the queue at the water station is 40 people deep.

> **6pm Sunday.** A storm warning is issued for 7pm. The Lawn Stage is
> open-air and packed.

Then think about the real constraints on the people involved:

-   **Mo** is on foot all day, with a radio earpiece and a phone in a
    pocket. Long screens and long messages don't get read mid-incident.
-   **Volunteers** are mostly students. Many are first-timers, some are
    only free on Saturday, and most won't read a long message.
-   **Radio traffic** is short, noisy and full of jargon, and it
    disappears once it's said.

These are prompts to think with, not problems you have to solve.

## 🧭 Possible Directions

Starting points, not answers. Pick one, combine a few, or go somewhere
else entirely.

### Building the Roster

Three hundred people, free-text availability, skills and certificates.
How do you build a fair roster that actually covers every role?

### Repairing It on the Day

No-shows, swaps, people who need a break in the heat. How does the
roster recover in minutes, and who gets asked to move?

### Making Sense of the Noise

Reports arrive by radio, text and word of mouth. How does Mo see what
matters first, without losing anything?

### Preparing for What Could Go Wrong

Heat, storms, crowding, lost children. What could be planned, rehearsed
or written in advance, before the gates open?

### Doing, Not Just Flagging

Could your product message the right twelve volunteers, draft the
incident report or move a team, with a person approving it? Where must
it never act alone?

## 🧪 Show It Working

Show your product working on realistic examples. If you need inputs like
volunteer availability, radio messages or a weather forecast, make up
believable ones.
